How the truck size estimate works

Short answer: the estimator adds up the packed volume of the items you choose, compares it with the usable cargo space of five common Indian goods-vehicle classes, suggests the smallest class that covers it, and then checks that every item has a physical place in that vehicle. Last reviewed November 2026.

The method, step by step

  1. You choose a home size. Studio / 1 RK to 4+ BHK, or not sure. This is context only. It does not add items and does not limit what you can add.
  2. You add items. Each catalogue item has variants (for example 3-seater sofa, or queen bed assembled or dismantled). Every variant has a packed volume in cubic feet and a packed size in centimetres.
  3. Volumes are added. Item volume multiplied by quantity, summed. A bundle (such as a bed set) counts once. A dismantled item counts once, using the volume of its variant, even though it is drawn as several flat parts.
  4. Vehicles are compared. Usable volume is internal length × width × height × 0.75. At or below 100% of usable volume a load fits by volume; at 80% or more it is shown as near capacity.
  5. The smallest class that fits is suggested. If your load is bigger than the largest class, the result says that more than one vehicle, or a manual survey, may be needed. It never claims that a single vehicle fits.
  6. A 3D loading plan checks placement. The suggested vehicle is tested in the background. If some items have no valid place, the next larger class that can take everything is suggested and the reason is shown.

Terms used in the estimator

Cargo volume
The space a load body offers, in cubic feet. It is approximate cargo space, not the weight a vehicle may carry.
Payload
The weight a vehicle may carry. This estimator does not check payload. Confirm it with the transport provider.
Internal cargo dimensions
The inside length, width and height of the load body. The estimator uses typical values for each class (see Truck sizes), not the dimensions of a particular vehicle.
Inventory
The list of items and quantities you add. It stays in your browser tab and is sent with a quote request.
Estimate confidence
An estimate is only as good as its inputs. Item sizes are approximate, vehicle sizes are class-typical, and custom items with a rough size are marked approximate.

How the 3D loading plan decides

  • Every item is placed as a box at its configured size. Nothing is shrunk, merged or hidden to make it fit.
  • Heavy and tall furniture and appliances stay on the vehicle floor. Fridges, plants, TVs, gas cylinders and every fragile item stay upright.
  • Nothing is stacked on a fragile item. A fragile item may sit on a suitable non-fragile one.
  • Items rest only on things that can carry them, and at least 70% of an item's base must be supported.
  • Items with no valid place are drawn outside the vehicle, and a larger or additional vehicle is suggested.

The preview is a visual planning aid, not proof of real-world fit.

What the estimate does not check

  • Weight, payload limits and axle loads.
  • Door, lift, staircase, lane or parking access at either end.
  • The real loading order, strapping, padding or centre of gravity.
  • The exact size and shape of your own furniture.
  • Items that need special handling: restricted items such as LPG cylinders are flagged for confirmation, not approved.

Final vehicle choice, space, payload and price should always be confirmed with the transport provider.
